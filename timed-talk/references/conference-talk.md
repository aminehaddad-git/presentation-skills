# Conference talk

A self-selected audience who can leave, in a room where the next speaker is waiting. Nobody is
grading you, and nobody has read anything you wrote.

## What differs from a defense

- **No shared context.** A jury read your report; this room did not. Budget real time for the
  setup a defense can skip, and cut depth to pay for it.
- **The clock is enforced by a stranger.** A defense chair may let you finish. A conference
  track lead will not — the next speaker is standing at the side of the stage.
- **Questions may not happen.** Do not park anything essential in Q&A. If there is a session,
  it is a bonus; the talk has to stand without it.
- **The audience can leave**, and the decision is made in the first two minutes.

## Structure

| Section | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 10% | The problem as the audience already experiences it. Earn the next ten minutes. |
| Why the obvious fix fails | 15% | What they would try first, and why it does not hold. |
| The idea | 20% | One sentence, then unpacked. |
| How it works | 30% | The mechanism, at the depth the room can follow. |
| Evidence | 15% | What happened when you did it. |
| Takeaway and where to go next | 10% | The one thing to remember, plus the link. |

**One takeaway.** A defense is judged on breadth of understanding; a talk is remembered for a
single idea or not at all. Write the takeaway sentence first and check every slide earns its
place against it.

## Delivering on the abstract

The audience chose this talk from a paragraph in a programme. That paragraph is a promise, and
breaking it is the complaint that shows up in feedback. Read the accepted abstract before
planning, and make sure the thing it promised actually arrives — early enough that someone who
came only for that gets it.

## Demos

Higher risk than in a defense: unfamiliar hardware, unfamiliar network, no second attempt. Play
a recording. If the talk is *about* live tooling and a recording defeats the point, rehearse the
failure path and budget the seconds it takes to reach the fallback stills.

## Slides

The layout rules in `SKILL.md` hold, with one relaxation and one tightening:

- **Relaxed:** a conference deck can carry visual identity. It is not being graded on
  conservatism, and a distinctive deck is remembered.
- **Tightened:** rooms are larger and screens further away. Push minimum body text up from
  24pt, and assume the back row cannot read anything you would call a caption.
